\#\# Hero direction

\- \*\*H1 must communicate:\*\* hearing-loss/hearing-difficulty relevance and the useful first step or benefit. Ensure “hearing evaluation” and “Altamonte Springs” appear in the H1 or immediately adjacent supporting line, so the topic and location are unambiguous. A warm slogan alone is insufficient. Prefer a direction such as understanding hearing difficulties and exploring help, rather than “hear perfectly again.”  
\- \*\*Supporting copy must cover:\*\* the clinic name, comprehensive evaluation, explained results, and options chosen from the findings. Use everyday conversation as a patient goal without promising a particular improvement. Add one short reassurance that hearing aids are not always the answer. Care in English and Spanish can be a compact supporting cue.  
\- \*\*Near-hero trust cue:\*\* \`5.0/5 on Google · 157 reviews\`, based on the research's October 12, 2026 screenshots. Recheck before launch. Keep provider credentials in the team section rather than crowding this cue with badges.  
\- \*\*Dominant action:\*\* \`Book Appointment\`, with a smaller call option. No “Free Test” button and no competing “Shop Hearing Aids” action.  
\- \*\*Keep out:\*\* a full symptom checklist, equipment acronyms, six manufacturers, staff bios, insurance promises, guarantee language, price promotions, automatic video/audio, rotating headlines, or multiple testimonial cards. The urgent-care notice appears visibly in the next section, not as tiny hero legal text.

\#\#\# S03 — Signs and appropriate urgency

\- \*\*Must retain:\*\* repeated requests to repeat; hearing speech but missing words; noisy/group conversation difficulty; TV/phone volume; a clear next-step explanation. Visible sudden-hearing-loss notice directs immediate medical care instead of routine scheduling.  
\- \*\*Optional:\*\* listening fatigue, phone difficulty, ringing/buzzing, or muffled/full ears, with no diagnosis. Use five or six well-distinguished signs rather than an exhaustive duplicate list.  
\- \*\*Emphasis:\*\* familiar experiences are worth checking; symptoms do not identify a single cause.  
\- \*\*Question answered:\*\* “Do these experiences warrant an evaluation?”  
\- \*\*Tone:\*\* empathetic and matter-of-fact. Avoid “Are you ignoring the warning signs?” or fear-based links to unrelated disease.  
\- \*\*Detail:\*\* descriptive one-line items plus a short explanation. Emergency guidance gets its own readable notice.  
\- \*\*Present visually:\*\* a checklist or simple grouped list; alert text with an icon and explicit wording, not color alone.  
\- \*\*Evidence:\*\* research §§2.2–2.4, 2.9; \`/hearing-loss\`. Retain the distinction between gradual concerns and sudden changes.

\#\# Copy style

Use everyday language, concrete details, and patient-centered “you” where natural. Lead each explanation with what matters to the person, then describe the method that supports it. Let headlines reveal the section's message rather than repeating “Our Services,” “Why Choose Us,” and “Learn More.” Use one idea per paragraph, meaningful lists, and descriptive link labels. Respect adults across ages and family circumstances; avoid patronizing language and automatic assumptions about retirement or frailty.

Use acronyms only after explaining them. A heading like “Measured in your ear, adjusted for your hearing” can introduce REM better than an acronym alone. Benefits should be supported by facts in the same section. Warmth comes from identifiable people, actions, and explanations rather than repeated claims of compassion.

Avoid generic AI phrasing, “state-of-the-art solutions,” “transform your life,” “unparalleled expertise,” “best in Orlando,” “journey to crystal-clear hearing,” fear-driven urgency, and unsupported comparisons. Do not repeat the complete symptom list, care promise, or provider biography across sections. Short contextual reminders near CTAs are useful; duplicate paragraphs are not.

Keep useful first-draft detail. If the page feels heavy, first improve grouping, paragraph rhythm, subheadings, and progressive disclosure. Record which facts would be layered or moved during Step 5 rather than quietly deleting them.
