\#\# Conversion and positioning

\- \*\*Primary conversion goal:\*\* an adult visitor schedules a hearing evaluation through the supplied appointment portal. The page sells a useful first step: understand your hearing, receive an explanation, and consider appropriate options. It does not presume a hearing-aid purchase.  
\- \*\*Primary CTA:\*\* \`Book Appointment\`. Every button with this label opens the booking popup, as required by the brief.  
\- \*\*Secondary action:\*\* call \`407-809-8154\` for scheduling, costs, insurance questions, existing-aid help, or mobile-service availability. This is the brief's number and remains the drafting default; its routing needs confirmation before launch because the public clinic number is different.  
\- \*\*Audience priority:\*\* adults noticing gradual hearing difficulty, then family members helping an adult loved one, then existing hearing-aid wearers dissatisfied with fit or performance. English/Spanish care and home visits remove practical barriers without changing the main topic.  
\- \*\*Value proposition:\*\* a comprehensive evaluation and understandable guidance from an independent local team, followed, when appropriate, by measured hearing-aid fittings and ongoing care.  
\- \*\*Trust proposition:\*\* identifiable licensed specialists, recommendations grounded in results, Real Ear Measurement, and specific patient experiences. Warmth supports professional credibility; it does not replace it.  
\- \*\*Conversion measurement:\*\* distinguish scheduler opened, booking confirmed, telephone link clicked, and directions clicked. A popup opening or call-link click is not a completed appointment. Use a confirmed portal completion event only if supported; otherwise report opens and clicks as intent metrics. Do not collect hearing concerns or other health details in analytics.

\#\#\# S03 — Recognize the signs; distinguish routine from urgent care

\- \*\*Purpose:\*\* connect familiar experiences to a sensible evaluation without diagnosing the reader.

\- \*\*Trust/CRO role:\*\* recognition without fear or blame. Include a plainly visible notice that a sudden drop in hearing needs immediate medical care rather than a routine booking. Do not hide this solely in an accordion or footer.

\- \*\*Imagery:\*\* generally unnecessary. Short descriptive items and restrained icons are clearer. If an everyday-life image is used, its role is recognition, not evidence of a patient outcome.

\#\#\# S04 — What happens at your hearing evaluation

\- \*\*Purpose:\*\* make the visit understandable and reduce uncertainty about testing and sales pressure.

\- \*\*Trust/CRO role:\*\* transparency is the central reassurance. “We will tell you if you do not need hearing aids” is supported; a blanket “no sales pressure” promise is not needed.  
\- \*\*CTA role:\*\* first substantial mid-page \`Book Appointment\`, after the visit has been demystified. Keep the cost qualifier accessible nearby or link to the cost FAQ.  
\- \*\*Imagery:\*\* helpful for explanation. Use one real sound-booth or results-discussion photo alongside a readable numbered process; do not add four redundant equipment photos.

\#\# Placement and interaction decisions

\#\#\# CTA repetition

Maintain the exact primary label and the same destination/behavior across S01, S02, S04, S05, S07 and S08. S10 supplies the final on-page booking opportunity. These repeats follow answered questions, rather than appearing after every card. Secondary telephone help is available in the hero, header, access/cost answers, location, final booking block, footer, and mobile call bar. Do not add quizzes, quote requests, product-shopping buttons, or competing WhatsApp/email actions.

\#\#\# Trust and review placement

Use a compact Google aggregate near the hero; disclose its source in visible text rather than using a bare unexplained row of stars. Use method evidence in S04/S05, accurate credentials with faces in S06, and the dedicated review section in S07. Keep important qualifiers beside the associated claim: costs beside evaluation-cost statements, five-year scope beside care, medical referrals beside options. Avoid a separate miscellaneous badge strip that repeats these facts without context.

\#\#\# Form placement and popup path

The inline scheduler lives in S10. Earlier \`Book Appointment\` controls open the popup immediately, without forcing a scroll or a preliminary form. Both paths use the identical clinic booking URL. Prefer one reusable scheduler instance whose state is preserved between presentations; if the build uses separate instances, verify that sessions cannot conflict and avoid loading both unnecessarily. Returning from the popup must not erase entered data or reset the reading position.

The popup has a named heading, keyboard-accessible close control, focus management, Escape-to-close, focus restoration, and usable internal scrolling. Its full-height mobile presentation keeps the close control available and hides the floating call bar. Add iframe title/dimensions and wrapper styling only as technically needed; preserve the supplied \`src\` and query string. If the portal fails, provide the clinic's \`/schedule\` page and campaign phone as explicit fallback choices. Cross-origin limitations must not be worked around by replacing the scheduler with an invented form. Test these behaviors during the build; this strategy does not claim the portal flow has been validated.

\#\#\# Location/map

Place S09 after objections and before final scheduling so a reader can resolve attendance logistics before booking. The full address remains readable independently of the map. Use the clinic's supported map embed for the same listing; the brief's place URL remains the directions destination. A place/search URL is not automatically a valid iframe embed. Lazy-load the map, retain the directions link when it fails, and never allow a blank map to remove the address/hours.

\#\#\# Why this order

The sequence moves from relevance and recognition to visit clarity, appropriate options, provider credibility, patient proof, remaining objections, attendance logistics, and booking. Ready visitors can book from the header/hero. Hesitant visitors gain enough information to choose. Provider stories and technical detail earn their place after the service is understood. Location supports attendance rather than displacing the main value proposition. The final conversion block closes the sequence without another promotional section.

\#\#\# Mobile priorities

Keep logo and booking in the sticky top header; omit desktop navigation and header phone when space is insufficient. Present hero copy, primary CTA, and trust cue before the image. Preserve readable body text, full address, cost qualifier, credentials, and urgent-care notice. Process steps become a vertical numbered sequence; image/text sections follow a meaningful reading order; no horizontally scrolling service or review cards. Three review cards remain available without a carousel. Accordions layer detail rather than deleting it. Map and scheduler load after the main content, with text fallbacks. Account for both sticky controls, zoom, keyboard, and safe-area insets.

\#\# Useful information retained through layering

Do not add separate sections for every condition, all seven services, six device styles, all device models, a technology catalogue, broad hearing-health advice, a brand wall, or an arbitrary stats band. Their useful parts have homes: symptoms in S03; testing in S04; REM/EAA, selection and aftercare in S05; provider detail in S06; cost/insurance, types of loss, tinnitus, existing aids and mobile care in S08; directions/hours in S09; preparation in S10. Additional clinically useful explanation can be an expandable subsection or a small related source link, provided it does not create a competing funnel. This is prioritization and layering, not an instruction to strip the first draft of substance.
