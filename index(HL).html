Signs of hearing loss

Do any of these listening difficulties feel familiar?

Hearing changes can be gradual. These signs can have different causes, including earwax. An evaluation helps you understand what's happening and what to do next.

See what your evaluation includes →

  • Asking for repeats

    You often need people to repeat.

  • Missing words

    You hear voices but miss words, or feel people are mumbling.

  • Difficulty in noise

    Restaurants, gatherings or the car make conversations harder.

  • Higher volume

    Others notice you turn the TV or phone up.

  • Harder phone calls

    Phone conversations feel harder to follow.

  • Listening fatigue

    Noisy conversations leave you feeling tired.

Other concerns to mention: ringing, buzzing or humming with hearing changes; muffled or full-feeling ears; or a long gap since your last hearing test—or never having one.

Sudden hearing loss? Seek medical care now.

A sudden drop in hearing over hours or days is a medical emergency. Seek medical care immediately—don't wait for a routine evaluation. Prompt treatment can improve the chance of recovery.

Ringing with sudden, severe spinning also needs immediate medical care.

Your first visit

Your hearing evaluation, explained step by step.

Hearing evaluations for adults. For anyone under 18, call our office to confirm age eligibility before booking. We explain each step, from your listening concerns to your results, so you know what to expect.

Select a step to see what your visit looks like.

  1. We talk through your hearing history, your overall health, and the situations where hearing is hardest for you.

  2. We check for earwax and other concerns before testing, and explain any care or medical referral needed first.

  3. In the sound booth, we test the sounds you can hear and how well you understand speech in quiet and background noise.

  4. We explain your hearing chart and what the results mean. A family member or friend is welcome to join.

More about the ear exam and hearing test

We use an otoscope to look at your ear canals and eardrums. If we find something that needs attention before testing, we explain the next step, including a medical referral when appropriate.

Wearing headphones in the sound booth, you listen for tones at different pitches to find the softest sounds each ear hears. Speech testing uses words in quiet and background noise. Your audiogram records your hearing across pitches.

If your hearing is within normal limits, we'll tell you that too.

Why we measure in your ear

Before fitting, electroacoustic analysis checks the hearing aid itself against the manufacturer's specifications. Real Ear Measurement then checks the sound delivered in your own ear. With the aid in place, a thin probe tube measures a calibrated speech signal at soft, average and loud levels.

The size and shape of your ear canal, and the fit of the dome or earmold, change how much sound reaches you. Two people with the same audiogram and the same settings can end up with different sound levels.

Fitting software relies on average ear canal values. Real Ear Measurement uses yours. It can also be used to check hearing aids you bought somewhere else. Jaysee is a HearingUp Certified Provider, a network built around best practices that include Real Ear Measurement.

Understanding Hearing & Treatment

Can hearing loss be treated or reversed?

It depends on the type. A hearing evaluation helps identify the type and degree of loss:

Sensorineural
Damage to the inner ear or hearing nerve. Common causes include age, family history, loud noise over time, some medications, illness and head injury. It's usually permanent, and hearing aids may help.
Conductive
Sound doesn't pass efficiently through the outer or middle ear, for example from earwax, middle-ear fluid, an ear infection or a perforated eardrum. It's often treatable, which may involve medical care, earwax removal or surgery.
Mixed
Both types together. The conductive part may be treatable, and hearing aids or other care may help with the rest.

Some findings need medical assessment, and we refer you when they do. A sudden change in hearing is a medical emergency. See the notice near the top of this page.

Will hearing aids help in noise?

Many hearing aids have features that can make speech easier to follow in noise, but no hearing aid removes background noise completely.

Familiar sounds and your own voice may seem different at first. Your brain needs time to adjust, and that varies from person to person. Follow-up visits over the first few weeks let us fine-tune the settings.

Can earwax cause muffled hearing?

Earwax is a common reason for muffled hearing, a full feeling in the ear, discomfort, or hearing aids that whistle. We offer earwax removal: a short health screening, a video otoscope exam you can watch on screen, gentle irrigation with body-temperature water at controlled low pressure, and a follow-up video check with home-care advice.

Irrigation isn't suitable for everyone, for example after ear surgery or with ear tubes, a perforated eardrum or a recent infection. If wax can't be cleared safely, we'll refer you. Call for cost details. Cotton swabs can push wax deeper, so it's best to avoid them.

Can you help with ringing in my ears?

Ringing, buzzing or humming often goes along with hearing loss. There's no universal cure, but some cases improve when an underlying cause is treated. For people who also have hearing loss, hearing aids, including models with built-in sound therapy, may make tinnitus less noticeable. Relief varies.

We start with a consultation, an ear exam and a hearing test. If your symptoms point to a medical cause, or you need more specialized tinnitus care, we refer you.

Get medical care right away if ringing comes with sudden hearing loss or sudden, severe spinning. See a physician promptly if it starts suddenly, is in one ear only, or pulses with your heartbeat.

How do professional and OTC hearing aids compare?

Over-the-counter hearing aids, and the hearing features on certain AirPods Pro models, are intended for adults 18 and older with perceived mild-to-moderate hearing loss. A hearing evaluation can help you compare your options.

Hearing aids purchased from our office include professional fitting and 5+ years of professional care, at every technology level.
