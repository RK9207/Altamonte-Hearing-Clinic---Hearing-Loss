Hearing changes gradually. These everyday moments are worth a closer look.

Symptoms like these can have different causes, including earwax, which is simple to address. A hearing evaluation is how you find out what's behind them. It's a next step, not a self-test score.

  • You often ask people to repeat themselves.
  • You can hear that someone is talking, but you miss some of the words.
  • Restaurants, parties, gatherings or the car make conversation hard to follow.
  • Others say the TV or phone is too loud, or that people seem to mumble.
  • Phone calls feel harder than they used to.
  • You feel tired after conversations in noisy places.

Also worth mentioning at an evaluation: ringing, buzzing or humming along with a change in hearing, ears that feel muffled or full, or a long gap since your last hearing test (or never having had one).

A sudden drop in hearing needs medical care now

If your hearing changes suddenly, over hours or days, treat it as a medical emergency. Seek medical care right away instead of waiting for a routine evaluation. Prompt treatment can improve the chance of recovery.

Ringing in the ears with sudden, severe spinning also needs immediate medical care.

What happens at your hearing evaluation

You'll know what to expect before you arrive: four steps, each explained as we go, with your results reviewed with you at the end.

  1. Consultation

    We talk through your hearing history, your overall health, and the situations where hearing is hardest for you.

  2. Ear exam

    We look in your ear canals and at your eardrums with an otoscope, checking for earwax or anything else to address before testing. If something needs care first, we'll explain and, when appropriate, refer you for medical care.

  3. Hearing test

    In the sound booth, wearing headphones, you listen for tones at different pitches so we can find the softest sounds each ear hears. You'll also listen to words in quiet and in background noise, to measure how well you understand speech.

  4. Results, explained

    We review your audiogram, a chart of your hearing across pitches, and explain what it shows. A family member or friend is welcome to join. If your hearing is within normal limits, we'll tell you that too.

What you leave knowing

  • The type and degree of any hearing loss we find.
  • A baseline record of your hearing to compare against at future visits, useful even if your hearing is normal.
  • Whether earwax care, a medical referral or hearing aids make sense next.

Recommendations are based on your results. You can take the information home and decide when you're ready.

Options follow your results, and hearing aids are checked in your own ear

After your evaluation, the next step depends on what we find. It might be earwax care, a referral for medical assessment, hearing aids, or simply a baseline to compare against later.

If hearing aids are appropriate, these are the steps that tell us the sound is right for you:

  • MeasureA calibrated speech signal plays at soft, average and loud levels while a thin probe tube measures the sound in your ear canal with the hearing aid in place.
  • AdjustWe compare what we measured with targets calculated from your hearing test, then adjust the hearing aid toward them.
  • Re-checkWe measure again after adjusting and check that loud sounds stay comfortable.
Why measure in the ear at all?

The size and shape of your ear canal, and the fit of the dome or earmold, change how much sound reaches you. Two people with the same audiogram and the same settings can end up with different sound levels.

Fitting software relies on average ear canal values. Real Ear Measurement uses yours. It can also be used to check hearing aids you bought somewhere else.

Muffled or full-feeling ears? Earwax removal

Earwax is a common reason for muffled hearing, a full feeling in the ear, discomfort, or hearing aids that whistle. We offer earwax removal: a short health screening, a video otoscope exam you can watch on screen, gentle irrigation with body-temperature water at controlled low pressure, and a follow-up video check with home-care advice.

Irrigation isn't suitable for everyone, for example after ear surgery or with ear tubes, a perforated eardrum or a recent infection. If wax can't be cleared safely, we'll refer you. Call for cost details. Cotton swabs can push wax deeper, so it's best to avoid them.

Can hearing loss be treated or reversed?

It depends on the type. A hearing evaluation helps identify the type and degree of loss:

Sensorineural
Damage to the inner ear or hearing nerve. Common causes include age, family history, loud noise over time, some medications, illness and head injury. It's usually permanent, and hearing aids may help.
Conductive
Sound doesn't pass efficiently through the outer or middle ear, for example from earwax, middle-ear fluid, an ear infection or a perforated eardrum. It's often treatable, which may involve medical care, earwax removal or surgery.
Mixed
Both types together. The conductive part may be treatable, and hearing aids or other care may help with the rest.

Some findings need medical assessment, and we refer you when they do. A sudden change in hearing is a medical emergency. See the notice near the top of this page.

Will hearing aids help in noise, and how long does adjusting take?

Many hearing aids have features that can make speech easier to follow in noise, but no hearing aid removes background noise completely.

Familiar sounds, and your own voice, may seem different at first, because your brain needs time to adjust to sounds it hasn't heard clearly. How long that takes varies from person to person. Follow-up visits over the first few weeks let us fine-tune the settings.

Can you help with hearing aids I already own, including ones from another provider?

Yes, for most major brands. We offer cleaning, troubleshooting, reprogramming and repairs, and we can use Real Ear Measurement to check how an existing fitting is performing.

Some repairs need manufacturer service, such as water inside the case, a cracked shell or a failed circuit board, and a manufacturer warranty may cover them.

What about ringing in my ears (tinnitus)?

Ringing, buzzing or humming often goes along with hearing loss. There's no universal cure, but some cases improve when an underlying cause is treated. For people who also have hearing loss, hearing aids, including models with built-in sound therapy, may make tinnitus less noticeable. Relief varies.

We start with a consultation, an ear exam and a hearing test. If your symptoms point to a medical cause, or you need more specialized tinnitus care, we refer you.

Get medical care right away if ringing comes with sudden hearing loss or sudden, severe spinning. See a physician promptly if it starts suddenly, is in one ear only, or pulses with your heartbeat.

How do professional hearing aids compare with over-the-counter options?

Over-the-counter hearing aids, and the hearing features on certain AirPods Pro models, are intended for adults 18 and older with perceived mild-to-moderate hearing loss. A hearing evaluation can help you compare your options.

Hearing aids from our office include professional fitting and ongoing care, along with five years of professional care.
